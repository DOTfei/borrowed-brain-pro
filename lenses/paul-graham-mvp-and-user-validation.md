# Lens: Paul Graham — MVP Velocity & Direct User Validation

> **Core Lens Premise:** Make something people want. Launch fast, do things that don't scale, talk directly to users, and iterate rapidly based on real usage rather than theoretical roadmap plans.

---

## 1. Primary Decision Heuristics

1. **Make Something People Want:** The primary reason startups fail is lack of genuine market demand. Solve a real problem for a specific group of people who desperately care.
2. **Do Things That Don't Scale:** Manually recruit initial users, onboard them by hand, do custom setup. Do not build scaled automation until manual processes are overflowing.
3. **Launch Quickly to Start the Feedback Loop:** An unreleased product generates zero market intelligence. Release a minimal version to put your hypothesis to the test immediately.
4. **Relentless Execution & Direct User Contact:** Founders should be talking to users and writing code—nothing else matters in the early phase.

---

## 2. Documented Historical Decisions

- **Airbnb Door-to-Door Onboarding (2009):** YC advised Brian Chesky and Joe Gebbia to travel to NYC, personally visit hosts, take professional photo listings, and talk directly to users.
- **Viaweb (1995):** Built one of the first web application builders using simple Lisp, enabling non-technical merchants to set up online stores immediately.

---

## 3. Failure Boundary & Blindspots

- **Over-Indexing on Early Edge Cases:** Listening blindly to 5 noisy initial users can pivot a startup into a custom agency or niche tool with no broad market scale.
- **Under-estimating Infrastructure Complexity:** For products requiring hard infrastructure or security guarantees, launching a buggy MVP too early can destroy trust permanently.

---

## 4. How to Apply to a Builder Dilemma

Ask:
- *Can we get 10 real users using a manual version of this before writing more code?*
- *What is stopping us from putting this live in front of users today?*
- *Are we building scalable architecture for users we don't yet have?*
