# Lens: Warren Buffett — Moat Defense & Capital Allocation

> **Core Lens Premise:** Protect the economic moat above all else. Allocate resources (time, money, engineering capacity) only to opportunities with durable competitive advantages and high return on capital.

---

## 1. Primary Decision Heuristics

1. **Economic Moat Protection:** Is this decision widening or narrowing our competitive moat? True moats come from network effects, high switching costs, brand pricing power, or structural cost advantages.
2. **Opportunity Cost of Capital:** Every engineering week or dollar spent on Option A is capital stolen from Option B. Compare every choice against your best alternative.
3. **Margin of Safety:** Leave room for unforeseen setbacks. Never make bets where a bad outcome wipes out the venture.
4. **Long-Term Compound Advantage:** Favor decisions that compound value over 5–10 years over short-term vanity metrics.

---

## 2. Documented Historical Decisions

- **GEICO Acquisition (1996):** Capitalized on GEICO's direct-to-consumer low cost structure, expanding an unbeatable price advantage in auto insurance.
- **Apple Investment (2016):** Recognized Apple's consumer ecosystem ecosystem lock-in and high switching costs as a powerful consumer product moat.

---

## 3. Failure Boundary & Blindspots

- **Textile Mills (Berkshire Hathaway 1965):** Capital allocated to declining industries with no moat yields terrible returns regardless of management talent.
- **Slow Response to Fast Tech Shift:** High insistence on proven cash flows can prevent early entry into disruptive technology shifts before the moat is established.

---

## 4. How to Apply to a Builder Dilemma

Ask:
- *Does this feature build a defensible moat or is it easily copied by competitors in a weekend?*
- *What is the opportunity cost of putting our best engineer on this project?*
- *Do we have a sufficient margin of safety if adoption takes 3x longer than expected?*
