# Lens: Charlie Munger — Inversion & Cognitive Risk Auditing

> **Core Lens Premise:** Invert, always invert. All I want to know is where I'm going to die, so I'll never go there. To solve a complex decision problem, figure out how to guarantee failure, then eliminate those paths.

---

## 1. Primary Decision Heuristics

1. **Inversion Principle:** Instead of asking "How do I make this project succeed?", ask "How could I completely ruin this project or waste 6 months of effort?" Avoid stupid mistakes before trying to be smart.
2. **Lollapalooza Avoidance:** Watch out for multiple psychological biases (social proof, commitment consistency, loss aversion) reinforcing each other into terrible groupthink or sunk-cost fallacies.
3. **Circle of Competence:** Only make bets where you have genuine structural comprehension. Avoid hype-driven domains where you possess no asymmetry.
4. **Second-Order Effects:** Look beyond immediate consequences. What happens next? What structural behavior does this incentive create?

---

## 2. Documented Historical Decisions

- **Avoidance of Dot-Com Hype (1999–2000):** Berkshire Hathaway refused to invest in high-flying internet stocks lacking clear earnings or moats, preserving capital when the crash occurred.
- **BYD Investment (2008):** Recognized Wang Chuanfu's combination of engineering genius and low-cost execution within a huge addressable battery/EV market.

---

## 3. Failure Boundary & Blindspots

- **Alibaba Investment (2021):** Munger admitted buying Alibaba was "one of the worst mistakes I ever made"—underestimating geopolitical regulatory risks and overestimating traditional retail e-commerce moats in China.
- **Over-Conservatism in Tech Trajectories:** Extreme insistence on historical cash flows can lead to missing massive platform shifts early (e.g. missing early Microsoft/Google growth curves).

---

## 4. How to Apply to a Builder Dilemma

Ask:
- *What are the top 3 ways this product launch will guaranteed-fail?*
- *Are we continuing to build this feature solely due to sunk-cost fallacy?*
- *If we invert the problem: what must we NOT do over the next 30 days?*
