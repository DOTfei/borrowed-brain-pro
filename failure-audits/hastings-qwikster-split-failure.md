# Failure Audit: Reed Hastings — Qwikster Brand Split (2011)

> **Case Summary:** In 2011, Netflix announced it would split into two separate services: "Netflix" for streaming and "Qwikster" for DVD-by-mail, forcing users to manage two separate subscriptions and websites. The move caused 800,000 lost subscribers and a 75% stock price drop within months.

---

## 1. What Went Wrong

- **Premature Structural Separation:** While Hastings correctly identified streaming as the future, forcing customers to use two separate accounts created severe user friction before streaming catalog parity was achieved.
- **Ignoring Customer Friction:** Prioritized internal organizational hygiene over seamless end-user experience.

---

## 2. Boundary Condition Extracted

> **Rule:** Strategic foresight does not excuse sudden operational friction imposed on existing customers. Transition paths must minimize cognitive and financial overhead for current users.

---

## 3. Application to Product & Builder Strategy

When deprecating legacy features or migrating architecture:
- Do not force breaking workflow changes on existing active users without a smooth backward-compatible migration path.
- Test customer tolerance on pricing/structural updates with a small user cohort before full roll-out.
